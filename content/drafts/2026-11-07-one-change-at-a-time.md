## 1) Telegram — text-only creator tip

🎯 CREATOR TIP: Change one thing at a time

If you change the hook, captions, music, pacing and video length all at once, you won't know which edit made the difference.

Try a simple experiment:

1. Pick one variable — for example, the first two seconds.
2. Keep the topic and overall format similar across a few uploads.
3. Compare early retention after each video has had a similar amount of time to collect data.
4. Keep the version that communicates the idea more clearly.

For a gaming Short, test whether showing the objective before the action makes the clip easier to understand. Don't judge the edit from one tiny sample, and don't compare videos with very different topics or upload ages.

## 2) Instagram — @j.k._knowledge carousel draft

**Slide 1:** CHANGE ONE THING AT A TIME

**Slide 2:** PICK ONE VARIABLE  
Test the opening, captions, pacing or length — not all four together.

**Slide 3:** KEEP THE COMPARISON FAIR  
Use similar topics and formats. Give each upload a comparable amount of time to collect data.

**Slide 4:** CHECK THE RIGHT SIGNAL  
Look at early retention and engaged views where available. Raw views alone do not explain why a viewer stayed.

**Slide 5:** LEARN, THEN REPEAT  
Keep the clearer edit. Test another variable in the next experiment. Results are not guaranteed.

**Caption:**  
A useful editing experiment should teach you something. Change one part of your next Reel or Short, keep the rest as consistent as practical, then compare the audience response after a fair period.

For gaming clips, one possible test is showing the objective before the action. Use your own analytics and avoid drawing conclusions from a very small sample.

## 1) Telegram — text-only creator tip

🎯 CREATOR TIP: Make the first frame make sense

A strong moment can still lose viewers if they cannot tell what is happening.

Before your next Short or Reel, check the opening frame:

1. **Show the context:** the game, device, task or result should be recognizable.
2. **Show the stakes:** add one short label such as the score, objective or question.
3. **Keep the action visible:** captions should help explain the moment, not cover it.

For gameplay, show the score or objective before the key move. For a tech explainer, show the device or result before the explanation.

Watch the opening on a small phone screen with the sound off. If the clip is confusing, simplify the first frame.

## 2) Instagram — @j.k._knowledge carousel draft

**Slide 1:** CAN VIEWERS UNDERSTAND YOUR FIRST FRAME?

**Slide 2:** SHOW THE CONTEXT  
Make the game, device, task or result recognizable immediately.

**Slide 3:** SHOW THE STAKES  
Use one short label: score, objective, question or outcome.

**Slide 4:** KEEP THE ACTION CLEAR  
Place captions away from the main action. Remove clutter that hides the subject.

**Slide 5:** TEST IT WITHOUT SOUND  
Preview the first two seconds on a phone. If the moment is unclear, simplify it.

**Caption:**  
A good opening is not only exciting — it is understandable. Give viewers enough context to know what they are watching, then deliver the moment your opening promises.

Try this on your next Reel and compare the early retention with your previous upload. Treat it as an experiment; results vary.

**Media needed:** Five 1080 × 1350 slides. Use a clean frame mock-up, one focal point per slide and large readable type. Use original shapes or a frame from the creator's own cleared footage; do not use an unrelated stock gameplay image.
